# SeaCommons — Editorial language and interface principles

Applies to FUTURES, public website, LIVE, PLAY, Docs, knowledge library and partner material.

## Voice
Write for a curious person first, an analyst second. Describe what someone can see, do or learn before describing the technology. Use concrete verbs, plain English and specific subjects. Avoid inflated claims, motivational slogans and templated contrast formulas (“not just X, but Y”, “from X to Y”, “empowering”, “revolutionising”, “unlocking”, “seamless”, “holistic”, “game-changing”, “cutting-edge”).

## Evidence
Separate **observed**, **reported**, **inferred**, **modelled**, **corroborated** and **proposed**. Never describe a proposed station, drifter or integration as deployed. Never imply an AIS gap proves deliberate concealment, or that a distress report confirms an outcome. Include date, source and limitations where needed.

## Interface copy
- Headings: short, informative and distinctive, not interchangeable marketing slogans.
- Supporting text: one useful explanation; expand technical details on demand.
- Actions: specific verb + destination (“Explore LIVE”, “Read how positions are checked”, “Offer a coastal site”).
- Icons: consistent and accompanied by visible text, never the only way to understand an action.
- Product imagery: use genuine, captioned screenshots and diagrams, clearly distinguishing concept images.
- Terms: explain AIS as vessel position broadcasts, VM as a virtual server, GNSS as satellite positioning, OSINT as research from openly available sources when first used in general-audience copy.
- Every technical section should link to the relevant Docs page when available. Never invent a documentation URL.
- Contribution prices are estimates or third-party prices, not a SeaCommons checkout. Do not collect card details, cloud credentials or private keys.

## Examples
Avoid: “Leverage cutting-edge geospatial intelligence to unlock actionable insights.”
Prefer: “Compare vessel positions with a dated report and see where the records disagree.”

Avoid: “A seamless contribution ecosystem.”
Prefer: “Choose the equipment or service you can provide. We will agree the test and access rules before connecting it.”

## Review checklist
Can a new visitor answer what this does, why it matters and what to click? Does every claim identify its evidence level? Are jargon and acronyms explained? Are links real? Does the visual add information rather than decoration? Is there a clear distinction between existing features and planned experiments?


## Prohibited frontend copy patterns
Do not use antithetical slogan templates: “X is not Y”, “not X but Y”, “not just X”, “more than X”, “X is a question, not a conclusion”, “small clues, bigger questions”, or similar formulaic rhetorical constructions. Express the actual observation, measurement, limitation or procedure directly. This applies to headings, cards, marketing copy, tooltips, navigation and future UI changes. Where a safety boundary is needed, state the concrete verification requirement and its reason without a slogan.
